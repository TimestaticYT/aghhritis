Aghhritis is an incurable condition in which the patient involuntarily says "aghhr" during ordinary movements. Each utterance advances a slow, irreversible metamorphosis into the Aghhr beetle bug. Scholars regard this outcome not as a complication but as the patient's destined fate, foretold since the disease's discovery.[1]

Signs and symptoms[edit]

The defining symptom is the aghhr event, a low, gravelly exclamation produced when standing up, bending down or reading a long email. Researchers have established a dose-response relationship: the more often a person says "aghhr," the more beetle they become. The average patient's total is tracked with the Aghhr count; roughly 40,000 utterances are thought to complete the transformation.[2]

Stages of metamorphosis[edit]

StageSigns
I. VocalOccasional "aghhr." Patient remains fully human and in denial.
II. GlossyFaint iridescent sheen on the shoulders; strong preference for damp corners.
III. AntennalTwo antennae emerge. Hats stop fitting. Wi-Fi reception improves.
IV. HexapodalFour additional limbs. Shoe costs triple. Speech reduced to "aghhr" in various tones.
V. FulfilmentComplete transformation into C. aghhrensis. Patients report profound inner peace.

The prophecy[edit]

The condition is named after Aghhr, a legendary figure said to have been the first to complete the metamorphosis. An ancient tablet attributed to him reads, in translation, "All who groan shall be shelled." Modern theologians interpret this to mean every patient is already a beetle in spirit, and the body is merely catching up.[3]

Treatment[edit]

No therapy can stop progression; at most it can be slowed:

  1. Silent standing therapy, in which patients rise from chairs with their mouths taped. Slows stage II by about four months.
  2. Anti-chitin moisturiser, applied twice daily. Delays shell formation but makes patients smell faintly of leaves.
  3. Vocal substitution ("hup!" instead of "aghhr"). Works until the patient forgets, which is always.

A 2024 trial of the drug aghhstatin was halted after every participant said "aghhr" while swallowing the pill, advancing a full stage.

Society and culture[edit]

Fully transformed patients form colonies known as Aghhr hives, where they live contentedly on compost and say "aghhr" without consequence. Advocacy groups argue the disease should be reclassified as a "career change."
